# AtollHost Privacy Notice — Draft for Legal Review

**Status:** Draft template. It must be reviewed and completed by the legal entity operating AtollHost before being published or used as a contractual notice. Do not present it as legal advice or a final compliance certification.

## 1. Who operates the service

AtollHost is the service name used in this draft. Before publication, the operator must identify its legal name, business address, privacy contact, and any required registration details. Do not publish this draft until those details are approved.

## 2. Scope

This notice describes intended handling of information processed through the AtollHost application and related guesthouse digital-operations services. The actual notice must match the deployed product, contracts, hosting region, vendors, and data flows.

## 3. Information that may be processed

Depending on features enabled, AtollHost may process:
- Account identifiers, names, email addresses, organization membership, roles, and security events.
- Property information, contact details, approved policies, amenities, and public-facing content.
- Enquiry and conversation information supplied by a guesthouse or guest, including message content and timestamps.
- Task assignments, reviews, draft responses, content, scanner findings, integration metadata, and audit events.
- Guest contact details only when the customer chooses to enter/import them and the feature requires them.
- Technical data such as request IDs, browser/device information, error events, and security logs.

Do not collect sensitive or unnecessary guest information by default. Customer organizations are responsible for ensuring they have an appropriate basis and permissions to provide guest data to AtollHost.

## 4. Purposes

Information may be used to provide authentication, property-scoped operations, enquiries management, task tracking, AI-assisted draft creation, support, security, integration operation, reporting, troubleshooting, and service improvement. Any analytics or model-training use beyond service delivery must be disclosed and separately assessed; do not assume customer data may be used for training.

## 5. AI processing

When AI features are enabled, relevant conversation context and verified property facts may be sent to the configured model provider to generate a draft. The production operator must disclose the provider, data sent, retention settings, geographic processing where known, and contractual safeguards. In MVP, generated guest-facing responses require human approval. AI output can be wrong and must be reviewed before use.

## 6. Sharing and processors

Data may be processed by infrastructure, database, email, monitoring, AI, and explicitly enabled integration providers. The operator must maintain a current vendor list and applicable data-processing agreements. Data must not be sold to advertisers. Do not share customer data with an integration that has not been configured and authorized for that purpose.

## 7. International transfers and location

Hosting region and cross-border processing must be confirmed and disclosed before launch. Do not claim data is stored only in the Maldives or any other location unless verified from the actual deployment and vendor configuration.

## 8. Retention and deletion

Retention periods must be defined per category before launch. The operator should retain only what is needed for service, contractual, security, and legal purposes, then delete or anonymize it according to an approved schedule. Backups and audit records may have separate retention periods that must be disclosed. A customer request does not automatically override applicable legal retention requirements.

## 9. Security

AtollHost is designed to use tenant isolation, access controls, encryption in transit, secret management, audit logging, and restricted privileged operations. No online service can guarantee absolute security. The published notice must accurately describe controls that are actually implemented.

## 10. Rights and requests

The final notice must specify the rights available under applicable law, how to submit access/correction/deletion/objection requests, identity verification steps, response timelines, and any complaint authority. Those details require jurisdiction-specific legal review.

## 11. Children

The service is designed for business operators, not directly for children. The operator must assess how guest messages and records involving minors are handled and define safeguards appropriate to the actual use case.

## 12. Changes and contact

The final published notice must include an effective date, change-notice process, and a monitored privacy contact. Do not publish until the operator identity and contact are finalized.

## Pre-publication checklist

- [ ] Operator legal identity and contact approved.
- [ ] Real data map and vendors verified.
- [ ] AI provider terms, retention, and data-processing settings reviewed.
- [ ] Hosting region and international transfer language verified.
- [ ] Retention/deletion schedule implemented and documented.
- [ ] Customer/guest roles and responsibilities reviewed.
- [ ] Applicable Maldives and other relevant laws reviewed by qualified counsel.
- [ ] Published notice matches implemented product behavior.
